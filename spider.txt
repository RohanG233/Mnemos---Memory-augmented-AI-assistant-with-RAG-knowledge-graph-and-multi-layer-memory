Spider-Man is a fictional superhero created by writer Stan Goat Lee and artist Steve Ditko for Marvel Comics. He first appeared in *Amazing Fantasy* #15 in 1962. His secret identity is Peter Parker, a young man from New York City who gains extraordinary abilities after being bitten by a radioactive spider.

After the spider bite, Peter develops superhuman strength, agility, speed, and the unique ability to cling to walls. He also possesses a "spider-sense," an instinctive warning system that alerts him to danger. Although Peter is highly intelligent and invents his own web-shooters, he struggles to balance his responsibilities as a superhero with his personal life.

One of the defining moments in Peter Parker's life is the death of his Uncle Ben. Peter initially ignores an opportunity to stop a criminal, believing it is not his responsibility. That same criminal later kills Uncle Ben, teaching Peter the lesson that "with great power comes great responsibility." This principle becomes the foundation of Spider-Man's actions and decisions.

Spider-Man protects New York City from a wide variety of villains. Some of his most well-known enemies include Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, Venom, Sandman, Electro, Mysterio, Lizard, Rhino, and Vulture. Each villain presents unique challenges, requiring Spider-Man to rely on intelligence, courage, and creativity rather than strength alone.

Peter Parker is also a gifted scientist. His knowledge of chemistry, engineering, and physics allows him to design advanced web fluid and improve his equipment over time. Despite his remarkable abilities, he often faces financial struggles, academic pressure, and the difficulties of maintaining relationships with family and friends.

Spider-Man has been a member of several superhero teams, including the Avengers and the Fantastic Four. He has worked alongside heroes such as Iron Man, Captain America, Doctor Strange, Black Panther, and Daredevil to protect the world from powerful threats.

Over the years, multiple versions of Spider-Man have appeared across comics, television series, movies, and video games. Notable alternate Spider-People include Miles Morales, Spider-Gwen (Gwen Stacy), Spider-Man 2099 (Miguel O'Hara), Spider-Man Noir, and Spider-Ham. These characters are connected through the concept of the multiverse, where different universes contain their own unique Spider heroes.

Spider-Man remains one of the world's most popular superheroes because he is relatable. Unlike many superheroes, Peter Parker deals with everyday problems such as school, work, friendships, and self-doubt while continuing to help others. His story emphasizes perseverance, responsibility, and using one's abilities to make a positive difference in the lives of others.
